Backspace rules in typing tests: should you correct mistakes?

Whether you may use the backspace key changes how you should type. It is worth finding out before the day of the test.

When backspace is allowed

You can correct a mistake, but every correction costs time. Fix an error immediately if you notice it as you type. If you notice it several words later, going back often costs more than the error itself.

When backspace is not allowed

Your mistakes stay. The strategy changes: type slightly slower, aim for accuracy from the first word, and never stop to think about an error you cannot fix. Keep your rhythm and move on.

Word-lock style rules

Some practice tools, including TypingNonStop, lock a word once you press space, so you cannot go back to a finished word. This trains you to commit and move on, which is useful for tests that do not allow corrections.

How to practise

Do most of your practice with the rules of your exam. If you do not know them, alternate: one run with backspace and one without. Practising without backspace builds calm and accuracy, and practising with it teaches efficient corrections.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to correct or to keep going?

If the correction is cheap and immediate, correct it. If it would break your rhythm or you cannot correct, keep going.

Do errors count more when backspace is disabled?

The test counts the errors that remain. Without backspace, every typo stays in the result.

How do I find the rule for my exam?

Read the instructions in your advert or admit card, or on the test screen before it starts.
